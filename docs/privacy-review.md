# Privacy notice review

Reviewed on 20 September 2026 against the repository and current primary sources. The original notice omitted browser preference storage and overstated what the analytics configuration guarantees. This review supports factual wording; it does not establish which national privacy laws apply to the site owner.

## Confirmed findings

1. **F1:** At review start, `src/components/Head.astro` loaded Google Analytics on the production hostname and set only `analytics_storage: 'denied'`. Google documents this setting as preventing first-party analytics cookie and app identifier access. It still sends measurements to Google. Advertising storage, advertising data use, and personalization have separate consent controls. A claim that the whole site sets no cookies does not follow from this setting alone. See [Google consent mode](https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/concepts/consent-mode).
2. **F2:** Measurements can contain timestamps, browser information, referrers, consent state, and a random number generated for each page load. State plainly that data still leaves the browser. Do not describe this as no data collection or complete anonymity. See [Google consent mode](https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/concepts/consent-mode).
3. **F3:** The original claim that Google models the counts is not established. Behavioral modeling requires at least 1,000 denied events daily for seven days and 1,000 users with granted analytics storage daily for seven of the preceding 28 days. Eligibility still is not guaranteed. This site's always-denied setting cannot supply the granted traffic itself; the property could include other data sources. See [Google behavioral modeling prerequisites](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11161109?hl=en).
4. **F4:** `src/layouts/Base.astro` reads and writes `localStorage` under the key `shortcuts`, with `on` or `off` values. Disclose this browser preference separately from analytics. The code gives it no expiry and does not send this value to a server. Clearing site data removes it. See the [implementation](../src/layouts/Base.astro).
5. **F5:** GitHub explicitly states that Pages records visitor IP addresses for security, including signed-out visitors. Keep this disclosure, state the purpose, and link to GitHub's policy. See [GitHub Pages data collection](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/what-is-github-pages#data-collection) and [GitHub privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).

## Recommended wording

1. **W1:** Use a neutral page description such as "How this site uses analytics, browser storage, and hosting data." Identify Leonid Svyatov as the site operator and keep the contact address visible.
2. **W2:** Say "Google Analytics measures visits and page use. Analytics storage is disabled, so Google Analytics does not read or write its first-party analytics cookies. Your browser still sends measurement data to Google." Describe the actual data and relevant controls without promising modeling results.
3. **W3:** Explain that Google receives the visitor's IP address and uses it for approximate location. Avoid a universal promise about IP retention: Google's [regional collection page](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11598602?hl=en) gives a general discard statement, while its [regional privacy page](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12017362?hl=en) expressly limits its guarantees to the EU, Switzerland, and UK.
4. **W4:** Add a clear link explaining Google's processing. Google's disclosure policy requires this information; its terms identify a prominent link to its partner privacy page as one way to provide it. See [Google disclosure policy](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7318509?hl=en), [Analytics terms, section 7](https://marketingplatform.google.com/about/analytics/terms/us/), and [Google partner privacy information](https://policies.google.com/privacy/partners).
5. **W5:** Explain any available analytics choice accurately. Google documents an opt-out browser add-on for `gtag.js`, but it does not stop hosting logs. An external browser add-on alone does not establish that the site meets any applicable consent or objection requirements. See [Google opt-out documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/181881?hl=en).

## Owner facts and remaining checks

1. **U1:** The owner confirmed residence in Armenia on 20 September 2026. The applicable rules for this site's audience and processing still need assessment. ICO guidance is a useful transparency reference, not evidence that UK GDPR applies. Where it applies, the notice must explain applicable rights, complaints, retention, recipients, and international transfers, in addition to identity and purpose. Keep the notice brief and accessible, use plain language, and review it when processing changes. See [ICO right to be informed](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-be-informed/).
2. **U2:** The owner confirmed a Google Analytics retention setting of 14 months and the account settings recorded below. Account user permissions and retention reset on new activity have not been checked. The retention setting does not govern all aggregated reports. See [Google data retention](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7667196?hl=en) and [Google data safeguards](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/6004245?hl=en).
3. **U3:** The owner accepted purpose-based email retention after clarification that it requires reviewing and deleting messages when their purpose ends. The notice states: "I keep correspondence for as long as needed for the purpose of the exchange." This records the agreed policy; actual mailbox retention has not been verified. Confirm the purpose and recipients of contact messages. The absence of an HTML contact form does not establish that the operator receives no personal information.
4. **U4:** Confirm production proxy and hosting configuration before naming another infrastructure provider. Cloudflare documentation was checked for availability, but its use for this site is not established by this research.
5. **U5:** Assess any consent requirement against actual behavior and applicable law. The comment "no banner needed" is not a result proved by denying analytics storage. Current ICO guidance covers technologies beyond cookies and includes a conditional statistical-purpose exception with safeguards. Do not assume that this site's analytics qualifies. See [ICO storage and access technologies](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-storage-and-access-technologies/) and [ICO exceptions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/).

## Does a personal site need this notice?

A short, accurate notice is appropriate while Google Analytics remains enabled. Google's disclosure requirement applies to Analytics users and does not provide a personal-site exception. This does not require a long template written for a large company. See [Google disclosure policy](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7318509?hl=en).

1. **A1:** Armenia's current law covers natural persons in Article 1. No general personal or family activity exception was found in the reviewed consolidated text. Calling the site personal does not establish an exemption. This is a bounded reading, not a legal determination for the site. See [Armenian law, effective consolidation from 15 August 2026](https://arlis.am/hy/acts/229131).
2. **A2:** Article 5(5) limits identifiable retention to the period necessary for its stated purpose. Article 19(1) requires destruction or blocking of personal data no longer needed for a lawful purpose. Indefinite email retention therefore needs a purpose-based assessment where the law applies; publishing the practice alone does not establish compliance. See [Armenian law, Articles 5 and 19](https://arlis.am/hy/acts/229131).
3. **A3:** Older official English PDFs are not reliable statements of the current law without checking amendments. Their Article 1(3) journalism, literary, and artistic exception is marked repealed in 2016 in the current Armenian text. This review does not establish a blanket website-notice format requirement. See [current consolidated law](https://arlis.am/hy/acts/229131).
